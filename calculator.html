How these are worked out

NRZ radius (m) = DSH (mm) × 0.012   [min 2 m, max 15 m]  AS 4970:2025 cl. 3.2
SRZ radius (m) = ((D × 50) ^ 0.42) × 0.64   [min 1.5 m]  AS 4970:2025 cl. 3.4

Read what changed in AS 4970:2025  ·  How to measure DSH properly

D is the trunk diameter above the root buttress, in metres. Encroachment is measured as a percentage of the NRZ area, not its radius, which is why a small looking incursion at the edge can still come out under ten percent.

What changed in 2025

Same maths, different words.

AS 4970:2025 superseded the 2009 version on 30 May 2025. If you learned this standard as TPZ and DBH, here is what moved.

Under 2009

TPZ did two jobs

One term covered both the figure that came out of the formula and the area you fenced off on site. In practice people used it to mean whichever they needed at the time, which caused no end of argument in planning responses.

Under 2025

NRZ and TPZ are separate

The NRZ is the calculated figure, the starting point. The TPZ is the physical area actually protected on site, which the project arborist determines after looking at the tree and the ground. The formula gives you the first. Your judgement gives you the second.

Under 2009

DBH, and loose encroachment

Diameter at Breast Height, which was never a defined height. Encroachment was handled largely as minor or not, with ten percent as the usual line and a fair bit of interpretation either side of it.

Under 2025

DSH, and three defined tiers

DSH is Diameter at Standard Height, measured at the same 1.4 m as before. Encroachment now sits in three tiers by percentage of NRZ area: minor at ten percent or less, moderate above ten and up to twenty, major above twenty or any SRZ intrusion.

What this calculator is, and what it is not

It gives you a reference figure, not an issuable one. TIMBR Field puts it more precisely than most: a calculated zone is reference only until a practising arborist attests to it, and no attestation is recorded here. This page does arithmetic. It does not assess trees. The NRZ is a starting figure, and whether it is the right protection distance for a particular tree on a particular site is a judgement for the project arborist, taking in species, age, health, soil, aspect and site history. Plenty of trees warrant more than the formula gives, and the standard expects you to say so.

The SRZ result assumes a single stemmed tree measured above the root buttress. Multi stemmed trees, leaning trees and trees on slopes need a different approach and a set of eyes on site.

These figures are drawn from published summaries of AS 4970:2025 rather than from the standard document itself, which Standards Australia sells.
